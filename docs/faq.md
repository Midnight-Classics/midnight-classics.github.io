# Frequently asked questions

## Do I need a computer?

No. The only time you need power on the bench is while you fit the needle, and then the USB-C socket is just a
power socket. A phone charger or a power bank does the job as well as a PC. Nothing is installed or run on a
computer. You can also power the module from the car instead, with the key on.

## What tools do I need?

Two flat-blade screwdrivers (a medium one for the case screws and a very small one for the faceplate screws), wire
strippers, a pin vise, a table fork from the kitchen drawer, and a USB-C cable with something to plug it into. A
drill press helps with the needle hub but is not required. The kit includes the one drill bit you need. Step 2 of
the manual shows them all.

## Where is my serial number?

On a sticker on the module's mount (the bracket on the back of the module). The mount ends up inside the
gauge case, so it is worth writing the number down before you fit the module in step 13.

## What does the fuse do?

The red wire has an inline fuse holder with a 2 A glass fuse in it. The fuse protects the wire. The board has its
own protection as well. Every gauge is fused the same way, so leave the fuse in the pigtail.

## Can I use it on another car?

The module is made to fit inside the Smiths RN 2351/02 tachometer, the one in the 1959 Austin Sprite.

On the wiring side, it is not limited to the Sprite's ignition. A switch on the back sets it for 4, 6 or 8 cylinders,
and the tach signal can come from the coil's negative terminal (points or electronic ignition), an HEI module's TACH
terminal, or the tach output of an ignition module or an engine computer (12 V or 5 V). It does not read an
alternator's W terminal or a raw sensor pickup. See [Wiring](wiring.md).

## Do I need the original mechanism?

No. The module replaces it. But do not throw it away: bag the mechanism and the old screws and keep them. Do not
take it apart.

## Does the conversion change my gauge for good?

Only in one place. You bore out the needle's hub so it fits the new shaft (step 8 in the manual). That cannot be
undone, so go slowly. Nothing else on your gauge is cut, drilled or changed, and the parts you take out are kept.

## Why doesn't the needle sweep when I turn the key on?

That is on purpose. When the needle is already resting on the zero pin, the gauge checks its position without
moving it, so it looks like the original.

## What does the needle do when I turn the key off?

It returns to 0 by itself. The gauge stores enough energy to bring the needle home after the power goes.

## Is there a warranty?

No. No warranty is offered with the kit. If something is not right, please [get in touch](support.md) all the
same.

## Will there be firmware updates?

Firmware updates are planned for later and will be published here. You do not need them to use the gauge.
